## The Question

Should BidFlow expand from software into actual construction execution? Should we become a contractor ourselves?

**Answer: No. Never.**

---

## The Katerra Lesson ($2.1B Lost)

Katerra raised **$2.1 billion** from SoftBank Vision Fund to vertically integrate construction — design, manufacturing, materials, assembly, everything. World-class leadership (former Flex CEO). Massive resources.

**Result: Bankruptcy in 2021.** Less than 6 years.

The failure wasn't execution — it was the thesis. Construction resists vertical integration because the variables are uncontrollable: hyper-local regulation, non-fungible labor, brittle supply chains, weather, bonding requirements, and razor-thin margins.

## The Numbers Kill It

| Dimension | Contractor Path | Platform Path |
|-----------|----------------|---------------|
| Year 1 capital needed | Rs 50L-2Cr | Rs 5-15L |
| Working capital cycle | 90-180 days | Monthly recurring |
| Net margins | 3-8% | 70-90% |
| 5-year cumulative profit | ~Rs 1Cr on Rs 5Cr+ capital | ~Rs 92Cr on Rs 2Cr capital |
| Risk profile | Concentrated, physical | Distributed, digital |
| Scalability | Linear (more projects = more people) | Exponential (more data = better for all) |

Construction margins are 3-8%. Software margins are 70-90%. The capital efficiency gap is 50x.

## The Thesis: Own the Brain, Not the Body

Every contractor needs:
- Finding work (tender discovery)
- Winning work (bid preparation)
- Managing work (project tracking)
- Getting paid (billing, compliance)
- Growing (working capital, reputation)

BidFlow owns the intelligence layer across all five — without ever pouring concrete.

**Shopify doesn't sell products.** It sells tools that let anyone sell.
**BidFlow doesn't build buildings.** It sells intelligence that lets any contractor build better.

Software scales. Concrete doesn't.
