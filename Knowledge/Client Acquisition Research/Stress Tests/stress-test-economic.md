## 2. Historical Precedent: AI/IT Spending During Crises

### 2.1 The 2008 Global Financial Crisis

**What happened to IT spending:**
- **Global IT spending declined:** Forrester predicted US IT market would dip to 1.6% growth in 2009 (down from 4.1% in 2008)
- **But automation ACCELERATED:** University of Pennsylvania economists Kopytov, Roussanov, and Taschereau-Dumouchel modeled how the recession led corporations to speed up technological adoption, particularly automation
- **Indian IT grew through it:** The Indian IT-ITeS sector maintained 13-14% CAGR even through the downturn, with export revenues growing from $10B in 2004-05 to $27.3B in 2009-10 (NASSCOM data). Growth slowed to single digits in FY2009-10 but never went negative
- **The outsourcing pivot:** After the slowdown, large Indian IT companies that hadn't focused on the domestic market pivoted, resulting in better growth in FY09-10. Offshore adoption continued as companies sought cost savings
- **European exception:** A 2021 study found German firms CUT IT spending, innovation, and R&D during 2008. Rigid labor laws may have prevented the automation pivot seen in the US

**Key data points:**
- Indian IT exports: $50.1B in FY2009-10, growing at 22.4% CAGR over 5 years despite crisis
- Dubai real estate crashed 42% from Q4 2008 levels; Abu Dhabi declined ~20%
- Abu Dhabi Central Bank injected $32.7B to ensure UAE bank liquidity
- Dubai World restructured $59B in debt; Abu Dhabi provided $10B bailout

### 2.2 The 2020 COVID Recession

**What happened to IT spending:**
- **Massive digital acceleration:** McKinsey: "5 years of digital progress in 8 weeks"
- **Remote work tools exploded:** Video conferencing, messaging, cloud services all surged
- **Indian IT surged in profitability:** While most sectors suffered losses, IT companies reported profit surges due to digital transformation demand
- **Fitch Ratings (Oct 2020):** Predicted pandemic would accelerate digital IT spend; impact would be "moderate and short-term"
- **Cloud computing outpaced every other tech category** (Gartner: IT spending became "recession-proof")

**Key data points:**
- Indian IT industry revenue reached $227B in FY2022 (15.5% YoY growth)
- IT industry grew to 8% of India's GDP in 2020, expected to reach 10% by 2025
- Singapore GDP rebounded 9.8% in 2021 after COVID contraction
- UAE GDP grew 7.5% in 2022 in post-COVID recovery

### 2.3 Pattern Summary

| Crisis | US/India IT Spending | Europe IT Spending | AI/Automation | Outsourcing to India |
|--------|---------------------|-------------------|---------------|---------------------|
| 2008 GFC | Slowed but grew; automation accelerated | Cut spending, innovation, R&D | Accelerated in US | Continued growing (single-digit dip then recovery) |
| 2020 COVID | Surged after initial freeze | Mixed; cloud grew, legacy shrank | Massive acceleration | Surged as digital transformation demand exploded |
| Pattern | Cost-reduction IT grows; innovation IT gets cut | More conservative; labor law constraints | ALWAYS accelerates during downturns | ALWAYS benefits (cost arbitrage increases) |

**Critical insight for Veyu:** Recession-era AI adoption is TACTICAL, not EXPERIMENTAL. Businesses double down on use cases with clear, measurable ROI -- automated screening, predictive scheduling, AI chatbots, cost reduction. They PULL BACK on moonshots and innovation labs. **Veyu must position as the tactical AI partner, not the R&D lab.**

## 5. The Outsourcing Advantage: Does India-Based AI Agency Benefit from Economic Stress?

### 5.1 Historical Evidence: Outsourcing During Recessions

**VERDICT: YES. Indian IT services have ALWAYS grown during global recessions, albeit at slower rates.**

| Period | Indian IT Export Revenue | Growth | Global Context |
|--------|------------------------|--------|----------------|
| FY2007 (pre-crisis) | $31.4B | 28% | Boom |
| FY2008 | $40.4B | 29% | Early crisis |
| FY2009 | $47.1B | 17% | Deep recession |
| FY2010 | $50.1B | 6% | Recovery begins |
| FY2011 | $59.0B | 18% | Post-crisis acceleration |
| FY2022 (post-COVID) | $178B (exports) | 15.5% | Post-pandemic boom |

**Key mechanism:** During recessions, Western companies INCREASE outsourcing to cut costs. The cost arbitrage (India vs. US/UK/AU) becomes more valuable, not less. Rupee depreciation during global crises AMPLIFIES this effect.

### 5.2 Current Rupee Dynamics

- INR has depreciated to ~90/USD (breached barrier in 2026)
- J.P. Morgan argues for continued INR weakness
- For Veyu: Every 1% INR depreciation improves margin OR allows more competitive pricing
- Price elasticity of Indian IT services exports: 1.2-1.7x (highly responsive to price changes)
- TCS case study: Currency devaluation historically enhanced cost advantages, won more contracts, expanded headcount

### 5.3 Counter-Arguments (Risks to the Outsourcing Advantage)

2. **BUT Veyu is not traditional outsourcing.** Veyu BUILDS AI solutions, it doesn't do traditional IT services/BPO. This is the opposite risk -- if AI disrupts traditional outsourcing, demand for AI BUILDERS increases.
3. **Second-order tariff effects:** US companies cutting discretionary tech spend due to their own tariff costs. Indian IT majors reporting single-digit revenue growth.
4. **Visa restrictions:** New US visa fees adding $100-250M in costs to top Indian IT firms. BUT Veyu works REMOTELY from India -- no visa dependency.

## 7. Special Analysis: Which TYPE of AI Spending is Recession-Proof?

### 7.1 AI Spending Taxonomy During Downturns

| Type of AI Spending | Recession Behavior | Relevance for Veyu |
|--------------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Cost-reduction AI** (automation, process optimization, labor replacement) | INCREASES -- IMF's Gita Gopinath: "The real wave of AI replacing jobs may happen during a recession, not boom times" | **PRIMARY POSITIONING** |
| **Revenue-generating AI** (lead gen, conversion optimization, pricing) | STABLE -- companies need revenue; AI that directly drives sales gets funded | **HIGH PRIORITY** |
| **Compliance/RegTech AI** (AML, KYC, fraud detection, regulatory reporting) | INCREASES -- regulations increase post-crisis (2008 → massive compliance wave). RegTech market growing from $15.8B to $82.8B by 2032. | **STRONG OPPORTUNITY** |
| **AI Infrastructure** (data centers, servers, chips) | VOLATILE -- depends on capex cycle; currently booming but bubble risk | **NOT VEYU'S MARKET** |
| **Innovation/R&D AI** (experimental, moonshot projects) | DECREASES -- first to get cut when budgets tighten | **AVOID THIS POSITIONING** |
| **Customer Experience AI** (chatbots, support, personalization) | STABLE -- cost savings offset investment; Info-Tech estimates 40-80% of routine L1 support automatable | **GOOD OPPORTUNITY** |

### 7.2 PE/Family Office Behavior During Downturns

**Private Equity:**
- 2025-2026: PE investing AGGRESSIVELY in AI. PE-led AI deals totaled $63B across ~300 rounds in 2025 (Crunchbase).
- Tech remains "resilient" per EY: "Limited exposure to tariff volatility, the intensifying race to lead in AI"
- BUT: Some PE firms halting or reducing software lending until AI disruption visibility improves
- **Key trend:** PE using AI for portfolio company value creation. Goldman Sachs summit: "Agentic AI as the biggest unlock for portfolio company value creation."
- **Implication for Veyu:** PE portfolio companies are ideal clients -- they NEED to implement AI, have PE firm pressure, but may lack internal capabilities.

**Family Offices:**
- 86% using AI in operations (Ocorian, Feb 2026 survey of 200 family offices with $119.4B combined wealth)
- BUT only 7% seeking AI investment opportunities currently (cautious stance)
- 65% planning to prioritize AI-related investments now or in future (JPMorgan 2026 survey of 300+ family offices)
- 74% expect to increase investment in AI and digital assets over next 3 years
- Family offices making "bullish bets on high-flying AI startups" even during Feb 2026 market selloff (CNBC)
- **Implication:** Family offices are users of AI, not just investors. They need operational AI tools. But investment decisions take longer -- 72% say most significant AI impact is 2-5 years away.
