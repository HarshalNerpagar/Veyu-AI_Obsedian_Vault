#### Strait of Hormuz Proximity to Dubai
Dubai is approximately 150 km from the Strait of Hormuz at its nearest point. The strait is 21 nautical miles wide at its narrowest. Approximately 75% of UAE crude oil exports transit through the strait. The UAE has partial mitigation via the Abu Dhabi Crude Oil Pipeline (ADCOP) to Fujairah (capacity: 1.5 million bbl/day), which bypasses Hormuz. However, roughly 1 million bbl/day of UAE exports remain blocked in a full closure scenario. (Source: Energy Industry Review; Deloitte)

#### Historical Resilience
- **1990-91 Gulf War:** Dubai's economy was partially stimulated by conflict (increased air cargo movements during Iran-Iraq war), though the Kuwait invasion reduced shipments. Insurance costs for Gulf shipping rose 1,000%. (Source: CSMonitor, Jul 1991)
- **2008 Global Financial Crisis:** UAE bank deposits dropped 25% during panic withdrawals. Dubai required Abu Dhabi bailout. (Source: MERIP)
- **2011 Arab Spring:** Dubai benefited as a safe-haven destination -- wealthy Syrians, Egyptians, and others moved capital to Dubai. Dubai's rise was "partly built on the instability of others." (Source: Daily Sabah, Mar 2026)
- **2020 COVID:** Severe tourism hit; Dubai pivoted to remote-work visa programs and recovered faster than peers.
- **2023-2025 Gaza/Israel-Iran conflicts:** Previous 12-day war in June 2025 did not significantly damage UAE infrastructure. The current 2026 conflict is qualitatively different -- direct strikes on civilian and economic infrastructure.

#### Business Confidence Impact
- DIFC has been evacuated by major firms (PwC, Deloitte, Citi). DFSA requested all firms complete business continuity forms by 13 March 2026. (Source: AIMA, Mar 2026)
- "The Iran war is a jolt to Dubai's business model" -- The Economist, 3 Mar 2026
- Headhunters are circling Dubai bankers; executives say "reasons for staying in the Gulf are becoming harder to justify" -- The Banker
- Goldman Sachs estimates UAE GDP could contract 5% if war lasts through April (Source: Al Jazeera, 17 Mar 2026)
- Allianz Trade estimates inflation in emerging markets could rise 0.8-1.0 percentage points from a two-month disruption (Source: Business Review EU)
- Real estate developers' share prices fell 13-17% in first week of conflict (Source: Business Review EU)

#### Decision-Maker Psychology
UAE-based founders and CEOs are in **survival mode**. The immediate priorities are: employee safety, business continuity, contract force majeure assessments, and insurance claims. Discretionary tech spending -- including AI services -- will be deprioritized in favour of operational resilience, cybersecurity hardening, and crisis management. This is not a market where Veyu should be prospecting in Q2 2026.

#### Veyu Implication
**PAUSE all UAE-focused outreach.** Revisit only after: (a) ceasefire achieved, (b) Strait of Hormuz reopened, (c) DIFC firms return to offices, (d) minimum 90-day stability period. The UAE will eventually recover -- it has done so after every prior crisis -- but the current conflict is the most severe test of Dubai's business model in its history. When recovery begins, there will be significant demand for AI-driven operational resilience, cybersecurity, and business continuity solutions.

### 2.2 UNITED KINGDOM (UK)

**Overall Geopolitical Risk Level: 5/10 (MODERATE -- Indirect Exposure)**

#### Current Situation
The UK faces compounding indirect pressures from multiple geopolitical conflicts but is not directly threatened militarily. Key stressors:
- **Iran war energy shock:** Bank of England revised inflation forecast to 3-3.5% for Q2-Q3 2026 due to oil price spikes. Rate cuts shelved. (Source: SysGroup, Mar 2026)
- **Russia-Ukraine ongoing costs:** UK continues to supply weapons/ammunition to Ukraine; potential peacekeeping deployment.
- **Defence spending surge:** UK targeting 2.6% GDP by 2027, 3.5% by 2035. MoD budget rising from £60.2B (2024-25) to £73.5B (2028-29). (Source: House of Commons Library)
- **S&P Global PMI:** UK manufacturers' input costs rose at fastest rate since 1992 in March 2026. (Source: SysGroup)

#### Key Risk Metrics

| Turnover improvement expectation | 49% of firms | BCC, Mar 2026 |

#### Defence Spending Crowding Out Commercial AI
This is a real but nuanced risk. The IFS analysis (September 2025) found that non-defence departmental spending will be "almost flat" between 2026-27 and 2027-28 as defence spending accelerates. EY-Parthenon estimates a £583 billion infrastructure funding shortfall if defence reaches 3% GDP, rising to £817 billion at 5% target. Defence programmes now represent 41% of unfunded UK capital projects, up from 16% in 2024. (Source: EY, Jan 2026)

However, commercial AI spending is largely private-sector driven and may not be directly crowded out by public defence budgets. The bigger risk is that higher energy costs, inflation, and interest rates reduce private-sector willingness to invest in AI transformation projects.

#### Historical Resilience
- **2003 Iraq War:** Limited direct UK economic impact despite military involvement. Business confidence dipped temporarily.
- **2011 Arab Spring:** Minimal UK economic impact.
- **2014 Crimea Annexation:** Modest energy price impact; UK economy continued growing.
- **2020 COVID:** Severe GDP contraction (-9.3%) but rapid policy response; tech sector accelerated.
- **2022 Ukraine Invasion:** Energy price shock contributed to cost-of-living crisis and UK's brief "mini-budget" financial crisis. UK proved vulnerable to energy-driven inflation but banking system remained sound.

#### Business Confidence Impact
- ICAEW Business Confidence Monitor was on course to turn positive for the first time since 2024 before the Iran war knocked it back. (Source: Reuters, 1 Apr 2026)
- Confidence has fallen for five consecutive quarters -- longest run in 6+ years.
- 52% of businesses cite utilities as a cost pressure; 35% cite energy price volatility. (Source: BCC)
- NCSC issued a formal cyber advisory to all UK businesses on 2 March 2026, citing Iranian state-linked threats. Iranian group Handala remotely wiped ~200,000 devices at Stryker (NHS supplier) on 11 March. (Source: SysGroup)

#### Decision-Maker Psychology
UK founders and CEOs are **cautious but still investing selectively**. The UK's distance from active conflicts provides psychological safety. Decision-makers are focused on: cost management, operational efficiency, and selective technology investments that demonstrate near-term ROI. There is a flight to "resilience-led" spending over "growth-led" transformation.

#### Veyu Implication
**UK remains a viable market but requires adjusted messaging.** Position Veyu's AI services around:
- Cost reduction and operational efficiency (not growth-led transformation)
- Cybersecurity and operational resilience (direct relevance given NCSC warnings)
- Near-term ROI with compressed timelines
- "Done for you" AI implementation that minimizes client effort during uncertain times

The UK market will reward agencies that can demonstrate tangible, rapid value -- not speculative AI experiments.

Australia's relationship with China is complex and evolving:

- Trade is now partially recovering: Albanese's July 2025 visit to Beijing yielded trade and innovation agreements (Source: China Briefing)

AUKUS's impact on commercial technology is "limited so far":
- Submarine construction doesn't begin in earnest until the 2030s

- Commercial spillovers are not yet materializing at scale -- Australia's small defence-industrial base and limited workforce constrain cross-over
- However, AUKUS Pillar 2 (AI, quantum, cyber, hypersonics) may create opportunities for dual-use technology firms

- **2003 Iraq War:** Australia participated militarily; minimal domestic economic impact.
- **2011 Arab Spring:** No significant impact on Australian economy.
- **2014 Crimea Annexation:** Limited impact; Australia's sanctions on Russia were symbolic.
- **2020 COVID:** Economy proved resilient; mining sector cushioned downturn.
- **2020-2023 China Trade Sanctions:** Australia diversified exports (redirected coal to India, barley to other markets) and proved more resilient than expected. Key lesson: Australian exporters can pivot when forced to.

#### Business Confidence Impact
Australia's USSC assessment (2026) notes the country "faces some major uncertainties" but has strengths in critical minerals, gas export capacity, and export diversification flexibility. AI strategy described as "risk-averse" compared to comparable countries, with Australia "unlikely to be a major AI player globally."

#### Decision-Maker Psychology
Australian CEOs and founders are **pragmatically cautious**. They understand geopolitical risk but are less paralysed by it than European counterparts. The mining/resources sector (Australia's backbone) benefits from higher commodity prices during geopolitical crises. Tech-sector decision-makers are focused on practical AI applications rather than frontier research.

#### Veyu Implication
**Australia is a stable, moderate-growth market for Veyu.** Positioning should emphasize:
- Practical, applied AI solutions (not frontier research)
- Supply chain diversification and resilience tools
- India-Australia time zone advantage (5-hour overlap enables round-the-clock services)
- Critical minerals and resources sector AI applications
- Compliance and regulatory technology (Australia's increasing security-driven regulation)

## 4. India-Specific Risks: How India's Geopolitical Position Affects Veyu's Market Access

### 4.1 India's Strategic Positioning
India maintains a policy of "strategic autonomy" (formerly "non-alignment"), which has implications for Veyu's ability to serve each target market:

- **India-UAE:** Strong bilateral relationship. India is UAE's second-largest trading partner. India's neutrality on Iran has been broadly acceptable to UAE. The India-UAE Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA) facilitates trade. Risk: If Iran war escalates further, India's balanced Iran policy could create friction with UAE's increasing alignment with US.

- **India-UK:** Growing economic relationship. India-UK FTA negotiations ongoing. UK welcomes Indian IT/outsourcing services. No significant geopolitical friction. India's record defence budget ($87B for FY2026, +15%) includes UK procurement partnerships. (Source: PR Newswire, Feb 2026)

- **India-Singapore:** Well-established economic corridor. Both countries maintain balanced US-China positions. Singapore's neutrality aligns with India's strategic autonomy. ASEAN framework provides institutional support. No significant friction points.

- **India-Australia:** Relationship strengthening under Quad framework. India-Australia ECTA (Economic Cooperation and Trade Agreement) in effect. However, Australia's deeper AUKUS alignment with US/UK could create complications if India's strategic autonomy is perceived as problematic by the Five Eyes intelligence community. Manageable risk.

- **India-US:** Most complex and highest-risk relationship for Veyu. Key threats:
  - **HIRE Act:** 25% outsourcing tax would directly impact Veyu's competitiveness
  - **H-1B reforms:** $100,000 fee for new entrants raises costs for hybrid delivery models
  - **"Buy American, Hire American" sentiment:** Growing political pressure against offshoring
  - **India's Russia ties:** India's continued oil purchases from Russia create periodic friction with US policymakers
  - However: India is also a key US partner in the Quad, a counterweight to China, and the US-India defence relationship is deepening

### 4.2 India's IT Outsourcing Industry Headwinds
Beyond geopolitics, India's outsourcing industry faces structural challenges:
- AI disruption: Indian IT stocks saw "unprecedented rout" over fears of AI replacing traditional outsourcing model (Source: BBC)
- India's $300B outsourcing industry faces existential questions about AI's impact on coding/BPO jobs
- Rising Indian developer salaries reducing cost arbitrage advantage
- New Indian labour codes increasing employer costs

### 4.3 Net Assessment for Veyu
India's geopolitical position is **broadly favourable** for serving UK, Singapore, and Australia. It is **increasingly risky** for serving the US market due to protectionist legislation. For UAE, the relationship foundation is strong but the market itself is currently disrupted.
