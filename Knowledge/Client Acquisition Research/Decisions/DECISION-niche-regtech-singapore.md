## 1. What RegTech Actually Is (Plain Language)

RegTech stands for Regulatory Technology. In plain terms: software and AI tools that help financial companies follow the rules.

Every bank, every fintech, every payment company, every fund manager, and every insurance company must follow rules set by regulators. In Singapore, that regulator is MAS -- the Monetary Authority of Singapore. Think of MAS as the financial police. They set the rules, they inspect companies, and they issue fines when rules are broken.

These rules cover things like:
- **Preventing money laundering (AML)** -- making sure criminals can't wash dirty money through legitimate businesses
- **Knowing who your customers are (KYC)** -- verifying that the person opening an account is who they say they are
- **Reporting transactions** -- telling MAS about suspicious activity
- **Monitoring for fraud** -- catching unusual patterns before damage is done
- **Following new regulations** -- keeping up as laws change (which happens constantly)

Right now, most of this work is done manually. Spreadsheets. Email chains. Human reviewers checking thousands of transactions by hand, one at a time. A compliance officer at a Singapore fintech might spend their entire day reviewing alerts that turn out to be nothing.

RegTech uses AI to automate this work: screening names against sanctions lists in seconds instead of hours, flagging only the transactions that actually matter, generating compliance reports automatically, and monitoring regulatory changes in real time instead of through manual newsletter reading.

The global RegTech market is roughly $20-25 billion in 2026, growing 17-22% per year. That number comes from averaging seven different market research firms (Grand View Research, Precedence Research, MarketsandMarkets, IMARC Group, Custom Market Insights, Future Market Insights, and Persistence Market Research). They don't all agree on the exact number, but they all agree on the direction: fast growth, no slowdown in sight.

**So what does this mean for Veyu?** This is a large, fast-growing market where most firms are still doing things the old way. That gap between "how things are done" and "how things should be done" is exactly where Veyu fits.

## 2. What Work Gets Done Day-to-Day in This Niche

To understand why RegTech matters, you need to see what a compliance officer's day actually looks like.

Picture a Head of Compliance at a Singapore fintech -- say, a Major Payment Institution processing cross-border transactions. Here is their day:

**Morning:** They open their monitoring dashboard (or, more likely, a spreadsheet) and see 200 new transaction alerts from overnight. Over 70% of these alerts are false positives -- false alarms that waste hours of review time. But they can't skip any of them. Regulators expect every alert to be reviewed and documented. So they start clicking through, one by one.

**Late morning:** A new customer applied for an account yesterday. KYC onboarding -- verifying who they are, checking them against sanctions lists, collecting documents. This process takes an average of 24 days when done manually. With AI, it could take 4 days. But this firm doesn't have AI. So they're emailing back and forth with the customer, manually checking government databases, and filing papers.

**Afternoon:** MAS published a new regulatory notice last week. Someone needs to read the full text, figure out what it means for the firm, determine what internal processes need to change, and write a memo for the CEO. A compliance officer does this by reading government gazettes and emailing their legal team. 77% of institutions still track regulatory changes this way -- manually.

**Late afternoon:** An MAS examination is coming up next quarter. The team needs to compile reports from spreadsheets, format them for submission, and make sure everything is audit-ready. Firms using manual compliance face 7x more examiner questions than those with automated systems. This firm will spend weeks assembling documents that a proper system could generate in hours.

Here are the numbers behind this picture:

- 38% of institutions operate with just 1-2 compliance staff
- Employee hours spent on compliance increased 61% from 2016 to 2023 (Bank Policy Institute)

**The emotional drivers:**

This is what makes Singapore RegTech not just a good niche, but an urgent one.

**Recent enforcement -- the fear is fresh:**

MAS imposed **S$27.45 million in fines against 9 financial institutions** on July 4, 2025, for AML/CFT failures connected to Singapore's largest money laundering scandal (August 2023). The specific firms and amounts:

| Firm | Penalty (S$) | Type |
|------|-------------|------|
| Credit Suisse Singapore Branch | $5,800,000 | Bank |
| United Overseas Bank (UOB) | $5,600,000 | Bank (only local bank fined) |
| UBS AG Singapore Branch | $3,000,000 | Bank |

| Trident Trust Company (Singapore) | $1,800,000 | Trust Company |
| LGT Bank (Singapore) Ltd. | $1,000,000 | Bank |

MAS's critical finding: **"Most FIs had established AML/CFT policies and controls. The breaches arose out of poor or inconsistent IMPLEMENTATION."** The 8 specific failures identified: CDD gaps, SOW not corroborated, beneficial ownership unknown, poor transaction monitoring calibration, inconsistent risk scoring, screening gaps, no ongoing monitoring, and weak compliance governance. This is an implementation gap, not a policy gap — exactly what Veyu's services address.

MAS also imposed **S$960,000 in penalties against 5 major payment firms** on June 27, 2025 for AML/CFT breaches (screening lapses, beneficial ownership inquiry weaknesses, systemic policy/control failures). This was the first time MAS publicly enforced penalties under the Payment Services Act. The message was clear: payment companies are now on notice.

MAS enforcement uses **composition penalties** — the firm pays the specified amount OR faces criminal prosecution. There is no negotiation. Historical precedent: MAS shut down two entire banks (BSI Bank and Falcon Bank) during the 1MDB case. 18 individuals were personally sanctioned in July 2025. MAS fines grew **579% in 2025**.

**What MAS is targeting in 2025-26:**
- AML/CFT compliance (the big one)
- Market conduct
- Technology risk management (which includes how firms use AI)
- Product advertisement compliance

**Specific MAS Notices by Entity Type:**

For Fund Managers (1,298 licensed FMCs): Securities and Futures Act, AML/CFT Notices (revised July 2025), Regulation 11C (adverse development notification, effective January 2025), VCC governance requirements, Liquidity Risk Management Framework.

For Payment Institutions (60+ MPIs): **MAS Notice PSN01** — Prevention of Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism for Specified Payment Services. Mandatory licensing, minimum capital adequacy, Singapore-based compliance officer, annual independent audits.

For Trust Companies (50+ licensed): **MAS Notice VCC-N01** (updated June 30, 2025). Enhanced CDD requirements converging with bank-level expectations.

**The July 2025 AML/CFT Revision — Specific New Requirements:**

1. **Mandatory Proliferation Financing (PF) assessments** — FIs must assess PF risk alongside ML/TF risk. This is NEW and most firms have no systems for it.
2. **SOW/SOF enforcement** — MAS now requires corroboration, not just collection. Singapore is positioning itself as a "global leader" in SOW enforcement (Fenergo analysis).
3. **Streamlined STR filing** — New electronic filing requirements, standardized formats.
4. **Ongoing monitoring** — Continuous, risk-based monitoring mandated (not one-off CDD).
5. **Record-keeping** — All CDD data, transaction logs, investigation records must be retained 5+ years.

**MAS AI Risk Management Guidelines (November 2025):**

MAS released its AI governance consultation paper on November 13, 2025 (consultation closed January 31, 2026). These will apply to ALL FIs and require: Board-level AI accountability, complete AI inventory (including third-party tools), AI risk materiality assessments, AI lifecycle controls (data quality, bias testing, explainability, monitoring), third-party AI vendor governance, and incident reporting procedures. This builds on MAS FEAT Principles (Fairness, Ethics, Accountability, Transparency) and the Veritas Initiative.

**MAS Inspection Process:**
- Risk-based approach: large banks inspected approximately annually
- Smaller entities (fund managers, payment firms, trust companies): inspected every **2-3 years**, plus targeted thematic inspections
- Off-site supervision includes capital reports, annual financial statements, compliance certificates
- On-site inspections check AML/CFT policy implementation, CDD file samples, transaction monitoring effectiveness, STR filing practices

**Financial incentives:**

MAS has a **$42 million RegTech grant scheme** to encourage adoption. The **MAS RegTech Grant** specifically covers up to **30% of qualifying expenses** (up to S$100,000) for FIs with <200 Singapore employees. Qualifying domains: KYC/CDD, transaction monitoring, suspicious activity detection, regulatory reporting, risk management, case management. The FI applies for the grant (not the vendor), and Veyu's fees fall under "professional services." The Enterprise Innovation Scheme offers a **400% tax deduction on AI spending, capped at S$50,000/year** (actual tax saving ~S$34,000/year). Combined, these incentives can reduce a client's net cost by 40-60%.

**The post-fine spending pattern:**

This is one of the most reliable patterns in financial services. Firms that get fined typically spend **2x-5x the fine amount** on compliance remediation over the following 12-24 months. Those 9 firms that were fined S$27.45M collectively? They will spend an estimated S$50M-$150M on compliance upgrades. Every one of those firms is a potential client or referral source.

**Budget timing:** Q2 (April through June) is active spending season in Singapore. Right now.

**So what does this mean for Veyu?** The timing is close to perfect. Fresh fines creating fear. New regulations creating urgency. Government grants reducing the cost to clients. Active spending season happening right now. And 131 RegTech companies in Singapore (per Tracxn), but most are SaaS platforms -- not custom AI implementation shops. ## 7. The Hyper-Niches Within RegTech

Not all RegTech is equal. Here are the sub-niches ranked by opportunity for Veyu, from best to worst.

### #1: AI Governance / Model Risk for Financial Institutions (Score: 29/30)

**What it is:** Building frameworks and tooling to govern AI systems used in regulated activities. Think of it as "compliance for AI" -- making sure the AI systems that banks and fintechs deploy are fair, explainable, auditable, and compliant with regulations.

**Why now:** The EU AI Act takes effect August 2, 2026. MAS is proposing its own AI governance standards. Every firm using AI in any capacity -- from automated lending decisions to fraud detection -- will need governance for that AI.

**The recursive demand angle:** This is subtle but powerful. AI governance itself requires AI tools to manage. Firms deploy AI for AML, then need AI to govern that AI, then need governance for the governance AI. It's a self-reinforcing market.

**Market size:** $500M-$1B globally, growing at 36% CAGR (Industry ARC).

**Competition:** Almost zero. Credo.ai is early. Prometai is early. 4CRisk was acquired by CUBE in 2026. No established player dominates.

**Veyu positioning:** "We build the AI governance framework your regulator will ask about in 2026."

### #2: Cross-Border Compliance Orchestration (Score: 28/30)

**What it is:** Managing compliance across multiple countries from a Singapore hub. A Singapore-based payment company operating in Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, Vietnam, the Philippines, and India faces different regulations in each country. Today, they manage this with separate teams or spreadsheets per jurisdiction.

**Why now:** BIS Project Mandala Phase 2 (November 2025) is automating cross-border payment compliance across MAS, RBI, and other central banks. Singapore firms operating across 6+ ASEAN jurisdictions need orchestration.

**Competition:** Very few purpose-built solutions exist. Most firms cobble together manual processes.

**Veyu angle:** India-Singapore corridor expertise is a natural advantage for an India-based team.

### #3: ESG Reporting Automation AI (Score: 26/30)

**What it is:** Automating the collection and reporting of sustainability and ESG (Environmental, Social, Governance) data for regulatory submissions.

**Growth:** 29.3% CAGR, with new mandates creating burst demand.

### #4: Regulatory Reporting Automation (Score: 25/30)

**What it is:** Automating the specific reports that firms must submit to MAS and other regulators.

**Veyu angle:** MAS has specific reporting formats that global vendors don't prioritize. A team focused on MAS-specific automation has less competition.

### Bottom of the List: Generic AML/KYC Automation (Score: 14/30)

This is the WORST choice despite being the biggest market ($4-6B). Why? Because 50+ funded vendors already serve it -- ComplyAdvantage, Sumsub, Fenergo, Jumio, each with $100M+ in funding. Price compression is brutal, the offering is commoditized, and clients see vendors as interchangeable.

**The counterintuitive move:** Target the SMALLEST market with the HIGHEST wealth concentration per client. AI Governance has a fraction of the market size of AML/KYC but virtually zero competition and every client is a large regulated institution with real budgets. Smaller pond, bigger fish, no other fishermen.

**So what does this mean for Veyu?** Start with AI Governance as the positioning wedge. It's the newest, least crowded, and most strategically defensible sub-niche. Use it to get in the door, then expand into cross-border compliance and regulatory reporting as the client relationship deepens.
