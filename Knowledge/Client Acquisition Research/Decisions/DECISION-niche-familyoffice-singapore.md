## 1. What a Family Office Actually Is

Imagine you're worth $200 million. You have investments in real estate across three countries, a portfolio of stocks and bonds, stakes in five private companies, a venture fund backing startups, some crypto, and a collection of contemporary art. You've got tax obligations in Singapore, India, and Dubai. You have trusts set up for your children. You give $2 million a year to charity. You're planning succession so the next generation doesn't blow it all.

Who manages all of this? You can't do it yourself. A regular wealth advisor at a bank handles maybe your stocks and bonds, but they don't understand your private company stakes, your tax structuring across jurisdictions, or your family governance issues.

So you set up your own private company — a **family office** — whose sole purpose is managing YOUR family's wealth and affairs.

A **Single Family Office (SFO)** serves one family. A **Multi-Family Office (MFO)** serves several families, splitting costs.

What they handle:
- Investments across every asset class (stocks, bonds, real estate, private equity, venture capital, crypto, art)
- Tax planning across multiple countries
- Estate planning and succession
- Philanthropy
- Risk management
- Regulatory compliance
- Family governance (keeping the next generation aligned)

Staff size ranges from 2-3 people (small SFO run by a trusted advisor and a couple of analysts) to 50+ for the large ones.

The minimum wealth to justify setting up a family office is roughly **$50-100 million**. In Singapore specifically, the MAS tax-incentive threshold starts at **$20M AUM** under the Section 13O scheme, which is why Singapore has attracted so many.

**So what does this mean for Veyu?** These are small, wealthy organizations with complex needs, lean teams, and — critically — budgets to pay for technology that makes their lives easier. They're not startups debating whether to spend $500/month. They're organizations where a $100K/year technology investment is a rounding error if it solves a real problem.

## 2. What Work Gets Done Day-to-Day

Let's paint a concrete picture. Take a typical Singapore family office with $500M in assets under management and a team of 10 people:

**The CIO / Investment Director** researches deals, manages portfolio allocations across stocks, private equity, real estate, and alternative investments. They're the strategic brain — deciding where the family's money goes.

**The Analysts (2-3 people)** are where the pain lives. They spend **60-70% of their time on manual tasks**:
- Pulling data from 5+ custodians, banks, and platforms into Excel
- Compiling reports that take 2-3 weeks to assemble
- Reconciling numbers across different systems that use different formats
- Manually extracting data from PDFs (capital calls, fund statements, K-1s)

**The COO / Admin** manages 15-30 legal entities across multiple jurisdictions (Singapore, India, Dubai, BVI, Cayman Islands), handles MAS compliance, coordinates tax planning with advisors in three countries.

Every month, this office processes **500+ documents**: capital call notices from PE funds, K-1 tax statements, quarterly fund reports, bank statements, trade confirmations. Almost all of these arrive as PDFs that need manual data extraction.

Reports that should take hours take **weeks** because data lives in 5 different systems that don't talk to each other. The CIO asks "what's our total exposure to China?" and the answer takes 3 days because someone has to manually pull data from a custodian portal, a private equity fund admin, a real estate management platform, and two bank accounts.

The research backs this up:
- **20-40% of working hours** go to manual data work (FundCount study)
- **72% of family offices** admit they are underinvested in operational technology (Deloitte)

**So what does this mean for Veyu?** The people doing this manual work are paid $90K-$150K/year each. Three analysts spending 60-70% of their time on automatable tasks represents **$270K-$315K per year in automatable labor** per office. If Veyu can automate even half of that, the ROI case writes itself.

## 12. Why This Is Phase 2, Not Phase 1 (Score Reduced from 84 to 65)

Three critical assumptions were overstated in the original analysis. Stress testing revealed:

### Indian Diaspora Advantage — Marginal, Not Transformative
- **No evidence** that Indian-origin FO principals prefer Indian vendors. Multiple research agents found zero data points supporting this claim.
- Cultural alignment provides approximately 30 extra seconds of attention in a cold outreach context — it doesn't bypass vetting, due diligence, or relationship requirements.
- Indian FOs have **HIGH due diligence standards** (Dakota data) — being Indian doesn't bypass their vendor selection process.
- Without direct personal connections (not just shared heritage), the advantage is negligible.
- The 10-20% meeting rate from diaspora contacts vs 2-5% cold only applies to **known personal connections**, not to strangers who happen to share an ethnic background.

### Cold Outreach to Family Offices — Near-Impossible
- **Near-zero cold email response rate** (1-3% at best for LinkedIn, effectively zero for email).
- **ALL major FO events are in-person and invitation-only** — no virtual attendance option.
- **No AI vendor has acquired FO clients through cold email campaigns** — every successful case study found (Addepar, Eton Solutions, Asora) started through either personal networks, ecosystem partnerships, or events.
- Addepar specifically started with smaller RIAs and moved upmarket to FOs only AFTER establishing proof points with dozens of clients.
- Most viable remote channel: partner with Singapore CSPs who set up FOs (IMC Group, Hawksford, Intuit Consultancy). But these partnerships take months to establish.

### Remote Operations — Cannot Attend Events
- Veyu cannot travel to Singapore for the EY Summit, Family Office Forum, or TiE events
- Without event presence, the #1 FO client acquisition channel is closed
- The remaining channels (CSP partnerships, LinkedIn content, warm introductions) work but are slow — 3-6 month timeline, not 55 days

### What Changes in Phase 2
Once Veyu has 2-3 RegTech clients and working case studies:
