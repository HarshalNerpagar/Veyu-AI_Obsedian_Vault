## 1. Money Distribution Inside PE

### The Concentration Pyramid (Global + UK Lens)

**The mega-fund consolidation is extreme and accelerating:**

| Tier | AUM Range | % of Total Capital Raised (2025) | # of Firms (est. UK) |
|------|-----------|----------------------------------|---------------------|
| Mega-funds | >$10B AUM | 45.7% of all capital raised | 8-12 (global firms with London offices) |
| Upper mid-market | $1B-$10B AUM | ~25% | 25-40 |
| Mid-market | $500M-$1B AUM | ~15% | 50-80 |
| Lower mid-market | $100M-$500M AUM | ~10% | 100-150 |
| Small/emerging | <$100M AUM | ~4.3% | 200+ |

**Critical data points:**
- The top 10 funds captured **45.7% of ALL capital raised in 2025** — up from 34.5% in 2024. This is the highest concentration in over a decade.
- Only **12.4%** of H1 2025 capital went to managers raising their first four funds, down from historical averages exceeding 20%.
- UK PE firms hold approximately **$190 billion (GBP178B) in dry powder** to deploy over 3-5 years (BVCA estimate).
- Total UK fundraising in H1 2025 fell by over 50% YoY to $41.6B. Most new funds raised under GBP50M.

### UK-Specific Landscape

**Top UK-headquartered PE firms by fundraising (2020-2025):**
1. **Hg Capital** — Largest UK PE fundraiser. $65B in funds under management. ~50 portfolio companies worth $135B+ aggregate enterprise value. **Heavily AI-focused.**
2. **Bridgepoint** — Second largest UK PE fundraiser.
3. **Inflexion** — EUR20B AUM, 130+ businesses backed. Named Mid-Market Firm of the Year EMEA 2025.
4. **Cinven** — Large buyout fund.
5. **Livingbridge** — Mid-market specialist, TMT-weighted.

**The mid-market is where the action is:**
- Deals in the GBP500M-GBP1B range represented **35.1% of total PE deal value** in 2025.
- Funds between GBP100M-GBP5B raised more capital than in the prior three years.
- TMT (Technology, Media, Telecom) is the dominant sector: 71% of Hg's portfolio, 46% for Livingbridge.
- Most sought-after sectors for exits: TMT, Financial Services, Industrials, Healthcare.

### Value Creation Budget Allocation

PE firms allocate value creation budgets across portfolio companies in several categories:
- **Technology/Digital transformation** — Now considered "the fastest and most reliable path to EBITDA upside" (Accordion 2026).
- **AI/GenAI specifically** — 60% of PE leaders plan to **increase AI/GenAI investment by up to 25%** in next 6-18 months; 8% plan increases of 26-50%; 2% plan increases of >50% (KPMG 2025 Survey of 500 PE leaders).
- Only 4% plan any decrease at all. **96% of PE firms are maintaining or increasing AI spend.**

### AI Spending Per Portfolio Company

**Enterprise AI spending data (cross-referenced):**
- Companies spent **$37 billion on generative AI in 2025**, up 3.2x from $11.5B in 2024 (Menlo Ventures).
- Digital budgets rose from **7.5% of revenue in 2024 to 13.7% in 2025** among large enterprises (Deloitte).
- IT spending growing at **4.6% YoY** in 2025, up from 3.5% in 2024 (BCG).
- For mid-market PE portfolio companies (GBP20-100M revenue), realistic AI/tech transformation budgets range from **$200K-$2M per year**, depending on ambition and PE firm support.

**Accenture's critical finding:** Every $1 invested in AI transformation delivers an **annualized EBITDA uplift of 2-4x** — a powerful multiplier effect at exit. This is the ROI story PE firms want to hear.

### The AI Operating Partner — The Decisive Signal

**This is the clearest signal of AI spending intent:**
- Large PE firms have begun introducing **AI Operating Partners** — full-time employees or part-time advisors (Korn Ferry 2025).
- The talent pool comes from three archetypes (Heidrick & Struggles):
  1. Previous PE experience at fund level in AI-specific roles
  2. ML and strategy consultants from Tier 1 firms with AI labs
  3. ML strategists from centralized AI labs in financial services
- **Berkshire Partners** (Jan 2026) appointed Richard Lichtenstein as Operating Partner, Head of Data Science and AI — reflecting the trend.
- **53% of PE firms** expect to hire more digital transformation specialists than in prior years; **51%** seeking more data scientists and AI experts (EY PE Pulse).

**The critical gap for Veyu:** Most mid-market PE firms (GBP100M-1B AUM) do NOT have an AI Operating Partner. They have generalist Tech OPs who are overwhelmed. This is the wedge.

### Hold Period and AI Spending Correlation

**Yes, longer holds = more AI investment:**
- PE hold periods are **extending** — firms holding assets longer due to challenging exit environments.
- Longer holds force firms toward **operational value creation** (including AI) rather than financial engineering.
- Global buyout dry powder hit **$1.2 trillion in 2024** — historic high. Capital waiting to deploy means firms must show transformation results.
- 2025 H2 deal volumes up 7%, marking strongest half since 2022, meaning fresh acquisitions that need value creation plans.

### The Widest Gap: Healthcare + Manufacturing Portfolio Companies

**Healthcare portfolio companies:**
- PE is heavily invested in healthcare (one of top 4 exit sectors in UK).
- Most healthcare portfolio companies have **no AI capability whatsoever**.
- Regulatory complexity means AI adoption is slower but the value is higher.
- AI for clinical operations, scheduling, and compliance = immediate ROI.

**Manufacturing/Industrial portfolio companies:**
- Korn Ferry specifically calls out: "Many lack the data infrastructure needed to support AI, and legacy systems, fragmented data, and cultural resistance all slow progress."
- AI sensors already showing results: SkyChefs used AI sensors to optimize inflight menus, improving meal profitability and reducing costs by 25% (Deloitte/SuperReturn 2025).
- Demand forecasting can boost revenue by **2-4%**; supply planning can cut inventory by **5-20%** (BCG).

### The Highest-Value Hyper-Niches

1. **PE-backed B2B SaaS companies needing AI product features** — Hg alone has 50+ portfolio companies. Each needs AI integrated into their product. Estimated 200-300 PE-backed B2B SaaS companies in UK that need AI product enhancement.

2. **PE-backed healthcare companies needing AI operations** — Compliance automation, patient flow optimization, clinical decision support. 50-100 companies in UK PE portfolios.

3. **PE-backed financial services companies needing AI compliance** — RegTech, fraud detection, KYC automation. Deep regulatory pressure driving spend. 30-50 companies.

4. **PE-backed business services companies needing AI delivery** — The largest sector by PE deal count (19% of Q3 2025 transactions). Process automation, pricing intelligence, workforce optimization. 100+ companies.

## 4. The "AI Knowledge Arbitrage" — Strategy vs. Implementation Gap

### The McKinsey/BCG Problem

**This is Veyu's single biggest opportunity:**

- PE firms are spending on **McKinsey, BCG, and Bain for AI strategy** — but these firms deliver PowerPoint decks, not production AI systems.
- BCG explicitly states: "PE firms should generally **buy mature third-party AI tools**" and recommends tools like Claude Code, Cursor, Clay, etc. — but **someone has to implement and customize them**.
- FTI Consulting notes: "AI is now embedded across the PE lifecycle, shaping portfolio value creation and exit planning" — but **65% of PE leaders marked AI as a top priority** while fewer than 15% track actual EBIT or revenue impact.

**The arbitrage:**
| What PE Firms Pay For | Who Does It | What's Missing |
|----------------------|------------|----------------|
| AI Strategy (GBP200K-500K) | McKinsey, BCG, Bain, EY-Parthenon | Implementation |
| AI Operating Partner (GBP250K-400K/yr) | In-house hire or fractional | Hands-on engineering capacity |
| AI Tools (GBP50K-200K/yr in licenses) | Vendors (Glean, Cursor, etc.) | Customization, integration, workflow redesign |
| **AI Implementation (???)** | **Nobody — this is the gap** | **Building, integrating, deploying, training** |

**Accenture's damning stat:** While 60%+ of portfolio companies claim to have an AI strategy, **under 15% track the EBIT or revenue impact** of their initiatives. Strategy exists. Execution doesn't.

### PE Firms Talking About AI But NOT Implementing

**Signals to watch:**
- Firms with AI mentioned in their LP pitch decks but no AI Operating Partner on the team.
- Firms that attended SuperReturn AI workshops but haven't deployed anything.
- Firms with "digital transformation" in their value creation plan but no AI-specific line items.
- Portfolio companies where the CTO has been asked to "figure out AI" without budget or team.

### The Fractional AI Head Opportunity

Tim Dietrich (industry commentator) identifies the core problem: "The candidate profile that PE firms are describing doesn't match the job they need done."

He proposes **fractional Head of AI** as the most practical path — and notes: "The PE model is already built for fractional and interim leadership. Interim CFOs, operating partners, functional advisors. These are standard tools."

**This IS Veyu's positioning:** Fractional AI implementation leadership for PE portfolio companies.

## 6. The "Stretched CTO" Pattern

### The Core Problem

**Portfolio companies with a CTO but no AI team:**

- Etonbridge Partners research shows **CTO appointments significantly outnumber CIO roles** in PE-backed businesses — CTOs are product/platform focused, not AI implementation focused.
- The most successful PE-backed CTOs "seamlessly blend technical excellence with commercial insight" — but they are **generalists**, not AI specialists.
- Mid-market PE firms are investing in **Technology Operating Partners** who work alongside portfolio CTOs — but these OPs are overloaded.

**Fortium Partners identifies the exact pain:**
- Mid-sized PE firms rely on **non-technical operating partners** to handle technology.
- Large PE firms have Technology Operating Partners but they are **overloaded with due diligence, portfolio company technology issues, and portfolio-wide initiatives**.
- The solution they propose: "Technology-Leadership-as-a-Service" — fractional CIOs, CTOs deployed to portfolio companies.

### Typical Tech Team at GBP20-100M Revenue Portfolio Company

| Role | Typical Headcount | AI Capability |
|------|-------------------|---------------|
| CTO/VP Engineering | 1 | Generalist, no AI specialization |
| Software Engineers | 5-20 | Can code but not ML/AI |
| DevOps/Infrastructure | 1-3 | Cloud/systems, no AI |
| Data Analyst | 0-2 | SQL/BI, not data science |
| AI/ML Engineer | **0** | **This is the gap** |
| Data Scientist | **0** | **This is the gap** |

**Result:** A CTO who knows AI is needed, has some budget from the PE firm's value creation plan, but has **zero internal capability to execute**. They're drowning in day-to-day engineering management AND being asked to "figure out AI" by the operating partner.

### Budget Reality

- These companies have budget for external AI. The PE firm's value creation plan typically allocates technology transformation spending.
- But they **can't find the right partner**: McKinsey is too expensive and delivers strategy not code; freelancers are unreliable; big consultancies don't understand mid-market constraints.
- Accenture confirms: only **25% of portfolio companies have a responsible AI policy**. They're flying blind.
